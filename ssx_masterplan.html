What Sunsex actually is
The Core Loop

You live → you capture (photo / video / location) → the system translates that into a cinematic isometric scene → that scene becomes interactive content you own, share, and inhabit. Life is the game engine.

What Makes It Defensible

It's not AI art. It's not a game. It's not a journal app. It lives in the gap between all three. The consistency of the visual system — the "Sunsex look" — IS the product. The pipeline is the moat.

The 3 things that must always be true
01 — Identity fidelity

The player is always recognizably themselves. Clothing, posture, setting. Not a cartoon. Not a fantasy avatar.

02 — Scene coherence

Every output belongs to the same world. Same camera logic. Same light. Same spatial grammar. No exceptions.

03 — Game readability

Any scene must feel like a playable level. Entry point. Primary zone. Hierarchy. Not just a pretty picture.

Core product layers
A — Map (World Layer)

Global, explorable Earth. Players = real people. Locations = real places. The world already exists — we render it.

B — Player (Identity Layer)

Persistent avatar identity. Evolves visually and functionally as the player lives. Clothing, location, activity all feed it.

C — Memory Engine (CORE)

Real actions → become scenes. Scenes = the actual content of the system. This is what makes Sunsex real.

Genre definition
Reflective Realism — a new genre

Part video game. Part storytelling tool. Part content creation engine. Inspired by drone photography, iPhone captures, and AAA open-world maps. The Sims meets GTA V exploration — but grounded in real locations, real actions, real identity, and a signature isometric perspective.

Core mental model
LIFE → PROCESS → SCENE → SYSTEM
Camera system — locked
H. ROTATION
45° left or right. Scene corner faces viewer. Two faces visible: front + one side.
LOCKED
V. TILT
-33° downward. NOT -45°. Exposes more front face than top. You see walls, not just roof. Critical distinction from standard isometric.
LOCKED
FOV
As flat/orthographic as possible. In 3D: 200–400mm+ equivalent. Camera pulled far back. No wide-angle barrel distortion. Lines stay parallel. No convergence.
LOCKED
VERTICALS
Vertical lines must stay vertical. No leaning, no keystoning. The clearest signal of the SSX camera. Non-negotiable.
LOCKED
FEEL
"Flattened perspective realism" — orthographic influence but not fully flat. Slight depth still reads, but controlled and clean.
LOCKED
Lighting rules — locked
KEY LIGHT
Soft, diffused, top-left direction. Natural ambient quality — as if shot on iPhone outdoors in overcast conditions.
LOCKED
FILL
Gentle overcast-quality fill. No hard second light. Shadows soft and directional.
LOCKED
SHADOWS
Present but soft. Ground shadow under all figures is essential for spatial grounding. Never harsh or dramatic.
LOCKED
MOOD
Neutral to slightly warm. Never cold/clinical. Never golden hour or night — unless scene demands it explicitly.
PER SCENE
Materials + surfaces — locked
BASE
Photoreal. Real-world textures: concrete, asphalt, wood, fabric, skin. Not illustrated. Not stylized. Not cel-shaded.
LOCKED
SIMPLIFY
Slightly cleaned. Remove noise, grain, compression artifacts. Keep the material — remove the mess.
LOCKED
SURFACES
Concrete, raw floors, wet asphalt, grass, stone, fabric, ocean — all valid. Surfaces define world type.
OPEN
Scene structure — every output must have all four
01 Entry Point

Where the eye enters the scene. Usually a corner, doorway, path, or edge element. Implicit but essential — without it, scenes feel disorienting.

02 Primary Zone

The main subject or action. Player character, key object, or focal event. Sits in mid-to-foreground. Should be identifiable within 1 second.

03 Secondary Detail

Supporting environment. Objects, furniture, architecture that give the scene its identity and world-building depth.

04 Background Context

Walls, sky, foliage, distant architecture. Sets mood and world scale. Can be simple — just needs to exist.

Character rules — from approved stills
SCALE
Figure is small relative to environment. World-scale matters. Character is never the full frame — they are one element in a larger scene.
LOCKED
IDENTITY
Clothing, hair, posture — preserved exactly from source photo. This is not a stylized avatar. It is the actual person.
LOCKED
PLACEMENT
Usually lower-left to center of frame. Grounded with shadow beneath. Never floating or without spatial anchor.
LOCKED
BODY ANGLE
Facing into the scene. Slightly turned. Back-facing or side-on reads naturally in isometric. Front-facing for hero shots only.
PER SCENE

AR camera movement system — design spec
📌 LOCKED SYSTEM NOTE

Parallel Camera / Phone Movement Control

The phone camera creates an invisible AR tracking layer that reads its orientation in space. That orientation data maps to the isometric character's position and facing within the scene. The user physically moves or tilts their phone → the character moves in the isometric world. The world itself parallax-shifts to reinforce depth.

Technical breakdown
Input — Device Sensors

Phone gyroscope + accelerometer deliver real-time orientation: pitch, yaw, roll. The DeviceOrientation API gives this in-browser at 60fps. No full AR plane detection needed for Phase A — just orientation tracking. Works today on Android. iOS requires a permission prompt.

Mapping Layer

Raw sensor angles → normalized movement vectors → character position delta in isometric coordinate space. Tilt left = character steps left. Tilt forward = character walks into scene. Center dead zone for idle stability. Sensitivity curve prevents jitter.

The Parallax Layer — the magic moment

As the phone tilts, scene layers shift at different speeds: foreground shifts least, background architecture shifts most. Creates convincing 3D depth inside a 2D isometric frame. Implementation cost: low. Demo impact: very high. This is the moment that makes a VC lean forward.

The pitch narrative

01 — The Problem

Your life is the most interesting game that's never been made. Every moment has the visual richness of a AAA open-world — but it disappears. Instagram captures the surface. Nothing translates the scene. Existing tools document. They don't render.

02 — The Insight

Gaming and real life are converging. Fortnite sells real clothing. BeReal and TikTok compete with games for daily attention. The next medium isn't AR or VR — it's life itself, rendered. No product has built this with visual consistency and identity fidelity at once.

03 — The Product

Sunsex takes your iPhone photo and outputs a cinematic isometric scene — your life as a playable level. Your avatar is you. Your locations are real. Your moments become scenes you can share, inhabit, and collect. Tilt your phone — your character moves.

04 — The Moat

The "Sunsex look" is the defensible asset. Two years developing a specific visual system — flattened perspective realism, game-logic composition, identity fidelity — that can't be replicated by prompting ChatGPT. This is a pipeline, not a prompt. A system, not a filter.

05 — The Ask

$50K pre-seed. 6 months to beta. 100 real users producing scenes daily. Use of funds: AI API infrastructure, dev acceleration, brand partnerships, beta community. Equity or rev share negotiable.

Where to look first
VC Targets

Gaming-adjacent funds. Creator economy investors. Digital identity / avatar plays. Consumer social with culture angle. Study who funded: Genies, Ready Player Me, BeReal, Locket Widget, Zepeto. Those investor networks are the target.

Brand Partners (faster money)

Streetwear brands. Travel lifestyle brands. Sneaker collabs. Pay for organic integration and case studies. One strong brand deal = proof point that unlocks investor conversations.

Priority order: (1) Lock visual pipeline → (2) Build one brand case study → (3) Use that to open VC conversations. Don't pitch before you have something to show.
